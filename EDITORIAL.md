# Editorial notes

This exhibition uses “almost” as a question, not a blanket verdict of failure.

Concorde operated a passenger service for decades. The Analytical Engine was never completed. Beach’s railway demonstrated a mechanism without becoming a network. Xanadu represents an alternative document model. These are different outcomes and are labelled separately.

## Evidence and interpretation

`promise`, `history` and `milestones` summarise the linked historical sources. `intro`, `legacy`, the obstacle classification, guided-walk groupings and all counterfactual fields are curatorial interpretation. Counterfactuals are possible consequences, not historical facts, forecasts or simulated probabilities. Changing one condition cannot establish that an invention would have succeeded.

## Known limits

Eight exhibits are a deliberately small first collection. Two references do not capture every cause, and obstacle labels simplify complex histories. The site does not claim a complete taxonomy, neutral causal ranking or a prediction that an abandoned technology will return. No live news or external model is used at runtime.
